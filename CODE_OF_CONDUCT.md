# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume they are acting in good faith, especially when
they are wrong. Nobody here owes you their weekend.

## What that means in practice

**Expected**

- Take the other person's point seriously before answering it.
- Give the context a stranger needs: what you ran, what you saw, what you use
  ReadPort for. Different libraries break in different ways.
- Accept that a maintainer may say no. A small project stays usable by
  declining most of what is asked of it.
- Let people be new. Everyone was, at the exact thing you are good at.

**Not acceptable**

- Harassment, insults, or dismissiveness aimed at a person rather than an idea.
- Comments about someone's identity, background, or ability.
- Sexual language or imagery.
- Publishing anyone's private information.
- Pressing someone to keep engaging after they have stopped.

## Scope

This applies to issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and
anywhere someone is representing the project.

## Enforcement

Report anything that troubles you to **ilan.kushnir@gmail.com**. Reports are
read privately and are not shared beyond what is needed to act on them. You do
not have to be the target of something to report it.

Depending on what happened, the response may be a private word, a public
correction, an edit or removal of the content, or a block. Persistent or
deliberate behaviour will get the last of those without much ceremony.

If you believe a decision was wrong, say so at the same address; it will be
reconsidered by the maintainer, who is also the person who made it. That is the
honest limit of a project this size — there is no committee.
